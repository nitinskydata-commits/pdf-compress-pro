How PDF Compression Works

A Portable Document Format (PDF) file contains diverse digital elements bundled inside a single stream: typography fonts, vector drawings, structural metadata dictionaries, and embedded bitmap images. Over time, documents often accumulate uncompressed objects, redundant full font families, and uncompressed high-DPI camera photos.

PDFCompress Pro applies a sophisticated, multi-stage optimization pipeline tailored to the contents inside each file:

🔤

Font Subsetting

Instead of embedding an entire 5MB font library, our engine analyzes your document and retains only the exact glyphs and characters actually displayed, drastically reducing payload size.

🖼️

Calibrated Image Resampling

Embedded photographs and scans are downsampled to optimal target DPIs (dots-per-inch) using bicubic interpolation and high-fidelity DCT JPEG encoding without degrading text readability.

🗜️

Object Stream Generation

Loose PDF dictionary objects are packed together into compressed stream containers using Flate compression, stripping redundant structural overhead.

Vector Text Preservation: Unlike basic tools that convert entire PDF pages into blurry images, PDFCompress Pro preserves vector text and fonts as scalable typography. Your text remains selectable, searchable, and crisp on Retina and 4K displays.

Which Compression Level Should You Choose?

Different PDF documents require different compression strategies depending on their destination. Here is our practical recommendation:

🟢

Low Compression (200+ DPI)

Best for: Architecture blueprints, digital art portfolios, photography books, and official scanned legal agreements where pristine visual clarity is critical.

🟡

Medium (150 DPI — Recommended)

Best for: Resumes, CVs, financial reports, business proposals, and everyday file sharing. Yields 40%–60% typical savings with zero noticeable quality drop.

🔴

High Compression (110 DPI)

Best for: Email attachments (staying under standard 10MB or 25MB email limits), university submission portals, and fast mobile document viewing.

⚫

Extreme Compression (75 DPI)

Best for: Strict government portal uploads (e.g. < 500KB or 1MB requirements). Maximally compacts embedded images while ensuring all text remains fully readable.

How to Compress PDF Files on Mobile Devices

📱

On Android (Chrome / Samsung Internet)

  1. Tap Select PDF File or drag your document into the upload area.
  2. Select your PDF from the Files app or Google Drive.
  3. Choose Medium (Recommended) or another target mode.
  4. Tap Compress PDF Now.
  5. Tap Download Compressed PDF to save the file to your Downloads folder.
🍏

On iPhone & iPad (Safari)

  1. Open PDFCompress Pro in Safari and tap Select PDF File.
  2. Choose your document from iCloud Drive, On My iPhone, or Files.
  3. Select your preferred compression level.
  4. Tap Compress PDF Now.
  5. Tap Download Compressed PDF, then tap the Safari Downloads icon to view or AirDrop your file.
